Who's First?

Help & Support

A tiny app for one fair decision: who goes first?

Choose with Chooser

In the native iPhone app, a fresh install starts in Chooser. The mode menu and Settings button sit in the iPhone's native top toolbar, where the system supplies the current glass appearance, placement, and accessibility behavior over the edge-to-edge board. Open the mode menu in the upper-left to choose Chooser, Tap In, or Pinball directly; the same menu can make the current mode your launch default. The Settings button in the upper-right opens eight visual worlds and an Information section with About Who's First?, the How It Works guide, Privacy, and Contact Support. The native Chooser playfield intentionally stays free of coaching text; guidance remains here and in that guide. The native app is iPhone-only and requires iOS 26 or later. The separate browser version still starts in Together and includes Together and Tap In.

  1. Put the phone on a stable surface.
  2. Have each person use one finger to touch the iPhone.
  3. Keep every finger down through the quiet 1.0-second stability window and the six-beat 2.75-second ring ritual.
  4. The final emphasized ring marks the person who goes first.

The native app uses large, broad material-authored rings and solid numbered board-game chits over the selected visual world's own surface. Chooser rings combine participant-hue inner and outer rims, same-hue relief, subtle material grain, tinted depth, and soft contact and cast shadows. They avoid a hard black outline, white crescent, neon glow, and idle pulsing. The winning ring, number, or seat supplies the visible result without an extra winner sentence; VoiceOver announces the complete result. You can change modes immediately while a group is safely being set up, collected, or displayed as a result. The current group clears without a confirmation step. Switching is paused only during a Chooser or Tap In countdown or while Pinball is running or actively revealing.

Choose with Tap In

  1. Open the mode menu in the upper-left and choose Tap In.
  2. Each player taps the screen once. The app adds a numbered entry for each tap.
  3. When everyone's in, tap Pick N. Pick becomes available after at least two entries are ready.
  4. After the one-second countdown, the emphasized number goes first.

Tap In supports up to 50 entries. Tapped twice? Use Undo to remove the newest entry; its number will be reused. Clear asks before removing the entire group.

Choose with Pinball

  1. Open the upper-left mode menu, choose Pinball, then place the iPhone flat.
  2. Have 2–12 people tap nearest where they are seated. Thin divider rays show each numbered seat's exact equal-area ownership without filling the regions or drawing an outer arena.
  3. When the two-pass wordless flick cue appears, flick across the playfield. Once the movement becomes a flick, the ball appears under your finger and follows it through release.
  4. The ball launches from that exact collision-safe release point in your direction. If ordinary bounces already reach the fairly selected seat, nothing changes. Otherwise, one localized section of the first wall visibly flexes and rebounds to redirect it.
  5. Where the visible ball stops is the result. Its endpoint and the winning pair of divider rays always agree.

Your flick controls the launch. Sometimes the first wall visibly flexes once so every seat keeps equal odds. The app commits the clamped release point, direction, and strength before securely drawing one equal-probability region. It first checks the ordinary reflected path. A natural match plays without any special effect; only a mismatch uses the theme-colored wall flex and its distinct springy tactile cue. There is no label, hidden spawn, or later steering, and ordinary wall impacts stay synchronized with their visible strikes. The focused result appears only after the same visible ball finishes, while its precomputed endpoint—not animation timing—owns the seat. Try Again keeps the same seating, and the same seat can fairly win again. New group clears every seat. The seat-count menu provides a nonspatial launch option for VoiceOver users.

Open a mode from Shortcuts

The native app provides Open Chooser, Open Tap In, and Open Pinball actions in the Shortcuts app and compatible system suggestions. Each action opens that mode for the current session without changing your saved launch default. Automations can use the matching local links whosfirst://mode/chooser, whosfirst://mode/tap-in, and whosfirst://mode/pinball.

Choose again

In Chooser, lift every finger and place them down again. The first new touch clears the previous result and starts the next group.

In Tap In, Try Again chooses from the same numbered group, and the same number can fairly win again. A background tap dismisses the result but keeps the group ready for another explicit Pick. New group clears every entry and leaves the app in Tap In for a different group.

In Pinball, Try Again keeps the same seats and readies another flick. A background tap does the same while dismissing the result. New group starts over.

Haptics and sound

The native iPhone version keeps Chooser's 1.0-second stability window tactilely silent, then uses six rising anticipation beats across one continuous 2.75-second physical arc. Every beat is synchronized with an escalating ring reaction, leaving clear tactile headroom for the longer wooden-token winner body. The choice winner has no synthesized tone. When Pinball needs its one fair wall flex, that visible rebound has its own springy tactile signature; ordinary wall impacts remain synchronized with visible contact, and the final settle uses a shorter distinct cue. The browser version uses sound where iOS does not provide web haptics. Make sure system haptics are enabled if you do not feel feedback.

Appearance and accessibility

Three-finger tap anywhere to shuffle the colors. One quick simultaneous tap by three fingers chooses a different visual world and saves it without changing the current mode, group, or result. The shortcut waits for a safe screen and never interrupts an active choice. With VoiceOver running, use the Shuffle colors action so iOS can retain its own three-finger gestures.

The native toolbar and playfield expose meaningful labels, hints, disabled states, and activation actions to VoiceOver; decorative texture does not create extra accessibility items. Larger text reflows Settings, including a single-column color picker at accessibility sizes. Reduce Motion skips nonessential transitions and the moving Pinball replay while preserving the same result. Reduce Transparency simplifies decorative layers, Increase Contrast strengthens piece edges and dividers, and Differentiate Without Color adds patterned winner outlines. Numbers and spoken results remain available independently of color.

Something not working?

Chooser needs at least two simultaneous touches; a mouse or trackpad cannot reproduce that experience. Lift all fingers, return to the chooser, and try again.

In Tap In, make sure at least two numbered entries are visible and finish the current tap before choosing. Opening Settings or its Information destinations—About Who's First?, How It Works, or Privacy—keeps committed entries. Leaving the app during a countdown cancels that countdown so you can pick again when you return.

In Pinball, add at least two numbered seats before flicking. Rotating or leaving the app during a run safely cancels the unseen result while keeping the seats, so you can start again.

Choosing another mode from a safe setup, collecting, or result screen switches immediately and clears the current group. There is no switch confirmation. Explicit Clear actions still ask before removing a Tap In group or Pinball seats. Mode switching is unavailable only while a choice is actively resolving.
