Who's First? Decide Together — App Review notes, version 1.1

WHAT CHANGED IN 1.1

iPad support. The app now ships for iPhone and iPad. This is a designed
layout rather than a stretched phone: Chooser rings, Tap In chits, Pinball
seats, the ball, and the ball's flight duration all scale with the board,
and onboarding chooses a one- or two-column page from the page's real
geometry. iPad is visual only. There is no Taptic Engine on iPad, and no
audio or haptic behaviour was added for it; the in-app Haptics and sound
screen states this directly.

Pinball's fairness mechanism is unchanged in kind and slightly widened in
where it can happen. Please read the paragraph below in place of the
Build 18 wording.

PINBALL FAIRNESS — CURRENT AND COMPLETE

Place the device flat and have 2 to 12 people tap nearest where they are
seated. Thin centre-to-edge rays show exact equal-area ownership. Once a
live gesture crosses the flick thresholds the ball appears directly under
the finger and follows it through release. The collision-safe clamped
release point becomes the exact analytic origin and the committed vector
the exact first leg.

Only after those player-controlled values are fixed does secure on-device
randomness select one equal-area region with probability exactly 1/N. The
winner is therefore drawn before any path is solved, and it is never
redrawn to make a path easier to find; if no path can be constructed the
round fails closed and the player simply flicks again.

The app first evaluates the ordinary reflected path. If its endpoint
already lies in the selected region, the path is unchanged and there is no
special effect. Otherwise exactly one contact on the path visibly curves,
compresses and rebounds once, redirecting the ball onto a genuine
reflected suffix that ends inside the selected region. A distinct springy
haptic matches that flex on hardware that provides one.

That flexing contact is a localized theme-colored section of a wall
wherever a wall on the path can reach the selected region. Where no wall
can, it is a seat ring the ball kicks off, which lights exactly as any
struck ring does. Walls are searched before seat rings, so a round that
could flex a wall always flexes a wall.

There is no text overlay, no hidden spawn, and no later steering. Every
other bounce is an ordinary specular reflection. The visible endpoint and
its two enclosing divider rays identify the same seat, so where the ball
stops owns the result, and render timing cannot change it.

WHY SEAT RINGS WERE ADDED

Fix a contact point and the distance left to travel, and the endpoint is a
one-dimensional curve on a two-dimensional board, so a single contact can
reach only some regions. A weak flick makes few wall contacts — about 5.6
on a 440x752 board — but strikes 13 to 23 seat rings. Before 1.1 some
regions were unreachable from some flicks. Because a failed round is
discarded and the next flick draws a fresh winner, a region-dependent
failure rate is rejection sampling and biases completed-round odds against
hard-to-reach seats. Searching seat rings removes it: measured over 2464
forced-winner trials per board, unreachable region/flick pairs went from
58 to 0 on iPhone 17 Pro Max and from 22 to 0 on a 13-inch iPad.

TESTING NOTES

A physical multi-touch device is required to fully test Chooser and the
three-finger colour shuffle. A physical iPhone is additionally required for
the Core Haptics cues, which iPad hardware does not provide. The app works
entirely offline, has no accounts, purchases, advertising, analytics, or
tracking, and stores only the preferred launch mode, the selected visual
world, a nonpersonal migration marker, and which one-time introduction
screens have been seen.
