## App Store copy

### Subtitle

Pick who goes first

### Promotional text

Choose with fingers, let a big group Tap In, or directly flick a fair Pinball. Private, offline, and made for iPhone.

### Description

Settle one question without an argument: who goes first?

Pick the way that fits the moment:

• Chooser — everyone places one finger on the screen at once
• Tap In — each person taps once to join a numbered group of up to 50
• Pinball — 2–12 people tap near their seats, then someone flicks a ball between clear equal-area dividers

Who’s First? pairs tactile board-game pieces with eight complete visual worlds. Each style changes the layered board surface, ink, controls, rings, numbered chits, dividers, and Pinball material—not just the participant colors. A simultaneous three-finger tap safely shuffles to a different world and saves it without clearing the group. In Pinball, the ball appears under your finger once the gesture becomes a flick, then launches from that exact clamped release point in your direction. If ordinary bounces already reach the fairly selected seat, nothing changes. Otherwise, exactly one contact — a wall, or a seat ring the ball kicks off — visibly flexes once to preserve exactly equal odds. Where the visible ball stops owns the result.

• Three playful ways to make one group decision
• Secure on-device randomness and mathematically equal Pinball regions
• True simultaneous multitouch in Chooser
• Numbered participants, so color is never the only identifier
• Eight complete board-game visual worlds, saved only on your device
• Three-finger color shuffle with a VoiceOver-friendly equivalent action
• A flat Five Seats, One Table icon designed for adaptive Home Screen appearances
• Portrait and landscape support
• Works completely offline
• No accounts, ads, analytics, or tracking

Great for board games, classrooms, chores, teams, family decisions, and any moment when the group needs one clear answer.

### What's New (version 1.1)

A first-run introduction. Who's First? now opens with a short three-page tour of Chooser, Tap In, and Pinball, so a new group can tell the three modes apart before the first decision. Each mode also shows a one-time card the first time you open it, and a single tap on the board dismisses it.

You can replay the tour any time from Settings → Show Welcome Again.

Everything still works completely offline, with no accounts, ads, analytics, or tracking.

## App Review notes

Who's First? is a focused, offline, native iPhone utility with three modes. Build 18 is iPhone-only and requires iOS 26 or later. A fresh install opens in Chooser. The one-time launch-default migration v2 resets an upgrade to Chooser once, then honors any later explicit launch-default selection normally. The edge-to-edge custom board is hosted in a native navigation stack. A semantic mode `Menu` in the leading toolbar position and a semantic Settings `Button` in the trailing position receive the system Liquid Glass treatment and system placement; both keep 44-point targets, accessibility labels and hints, and accurate disabled states. The mode menu provides direct Chooser, Tap In, and Pinball commands plus an explicit command that saves the current mode as the next launch default. Settings contains eight complete visual worlds—Wingspan Original, Wingspan Americas, Wingspan Asia, Nidavellir, Concordia, Everdell, Lizard Wizard, and Leaders—and an **Information** section with About Who's First?, How It Works, Privacy, and Contact Support. A world supplies the layered board surface and procedural texture, ink, chrome tint, participant material, dividers, and Pinball ball treatment. A simultaneous three-finger tap in any safe screen chooses one of the other seven worlds, applies it without clearing the current mode/group/result, and saves it through that same local preference. The gesture is ignored during active or unsafe interaction, and VoiceOver users receive an equivalent Shuffle colors action. In a safe setup, collecting, or result state, selecting another mode changes immediately and clears the current group without confirmation. The mode control is disabled only while a Chooser/Tap In draw resolves or Pinball is running or actively revealing. Explicit Clear actions still ask for confirmation.

Chooser: place two or more fingers on the iPhone screen simultaneously and keep them down. The uncluttered playfield intentionally shows no coaching sentence during interaction. Large, broad rings use the selected world's material and expose its board surface through their centers. Their participant-hue outer and inner rims, same-hue relief, material-specific procedural grain, tinted occlusion, and deeper contact and cast shadows give each marker physical depth without a hard black keyline, white crescent, neon treatment, or idle pulse. The app allows a tactilely silent 1.0-second stability window for late participants, then runs a 2.75-second six-beat physical anticipation arc at 0.24, 0.78, 1.28, 1.72, 2.10, and 2.43 seconds. Its final anticipation strength is capped at 0.74, leaving 320 milliseconds of tactile headroom before the result. Core Haptics playback begins before the visual winner clock is anchored; tactile contact lands at exact maximum compression 0.082 seconds into the 0.82-second visual landing, followed by the 0.78-second low-sharpness body. The winning ring supplies the visible result without an additional winner sentence; VoiceOver receives the full result. Lift every finger and place them again for a new round; the first new touch also clears the previous result.

Tap In: open the mode menu and choose Tap In, then have each participant tap once. The app creates sequential numbered entries as solid board-game chits. After at least two entries are committed, tap Pick N for a one-second countdown and an on-device random result. Try Again draws from the same group and may repeat a previous winner. A background tap dismisses the winner emphasis while preserving the full group for another explicit Pick. Undo removes the newest entry, Clear requires confirmation, and New group clears all entries while staying in Tap In. The limit is 50 entries.

Pinball: open the mode menu and choose Pinball. Place the iPhone flat and have 2–12 people tap nearest where they are seated. Thin center-to-edge rays show the exact equal-area ownership boundaries without filling territories or outlining the outer playfield. After the second seat settles, a brief wordless fingertip-and-streak demonstration indicates that the surface is ready for a flick. During a live gesture, the 30-point ball appears directly beneath the finger as soon as the movement crosses the committed flick thresholds. It follows the finger without an animated transfer. On release, the collision-safe clamped ball center becomes the exact analytic launch origin and the committed velocity becomes the exact first-leg direction. Only after those player-controlled values are fixed does secure on-device randomness select one region with exactly `1/N` probability. The app first evaluates the natural specular path. If its endpoint already lies in the selected region, the path is unchanged and there is no special effect. Otherwise, exactly one contact on the path visibly curves, compresses, and rebounds once while redirecting the outgoing path onto a genuine reflected suffix ending inside that region; a distinct springy haptic matches the flex. That contact is a localized theme-colored section of a wall wherever a wall can reach the selected region, and otherwise a seat ring the ball kicks off, which lights exactly as any struck ring does. Seat rings are searched only after every wall, so a path that could flex a wall always does. There is no text overlay, hidden spawn, or later steering. Every later wall bounce is an ordinary specular reflection. The endpoint and its two enclosing divider rays identify the same winner, so the visible landing owns the result. Try Again keeps the seats and permits a repeat winner; a background tap dismisses the result and readies another flick with the same seats, while New group clears them. The accessible seat-count menu provides a nonspatial setup option. Reduce Motion presents the same selected endpoint without replaying the moving flight.

The Settings Information flow, including About, How It Works, and Privacy, is bundled and remains available without a connection. The public Support and Privacy links open only when the reviewer explicitly selects them. Build 18 also publishes Open Chooser, Open Tap In, and Open Pinball App Shortcuts. They use the local `whosfirst://mode/chooser`, `whosfirst://mode/tap-in`, and `whosfirst://mode/pinball` deep links to select the current session mode without changing the saved launch default.

The custom interaction surfaces remain semantically accessible buttons with labels, hints, activation actions, and disabled traits. Decorative board drawing is hidden from assistive technology, while rings, numbered chits, controls, and results expose meaningful semantics. The visual system responds to Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, Differentiate Without Color, Dynamic Type, and each world's compatible light or dark control appearance. Differentiate Without Color adds segmented or dashed winner cues; participant numbers and VoiceOver wording remain available independently of hue. Build 15 remains the latest dedicated full-system accessibility audit; Build 18's final AppUITest suite and owner device review also pass with the new theme-shuffle accessibility path.

The app icon uses a brand-neutral, flat Five Seats, One Table composition: five equal player spots surround a two-plane circular table and neutral chooser puck. Stable Icon Composer 1.6 exports its layered source as Default, Dark, Clear Light, Clear Dark, and Tinted Dark appearances, with monochrome annotations for system treatments, without realistic texture, bevels, baked shadows, text, logos, or third-party game artwork.

No login, required network connection, permissions, purchases, advertising, or data collection are involved. Finger positions, Tap In entries, Pinball seats, flicks, paths, and results are temporary in-memory interaction state only; they are not names, persisted profiles, or transmitted data. App Shortcut and deep-link requests contain only the requested mode and are handled locally. Only the user's preferred launch mode, selected visual world, nonpersonal migration-version marker, and a nonpersonal record of which one-time introduction screens have been seen are stored locally. Theme shuffling changes that existing selected-world value; it adds no storage category, identifier, networking, analytics, or privacy declaration. A physical multi-touch device is required to fully test Chooser and the three-finger shortcut; a physical iPhone is additionally required for the semantic Core Haptics cues, which iPad hardware does not provide. Chooser keeps its 1.0-second stability window tactilely silent, then uses six rising anticipation transients across 2.75 seconds, capped at 0.74 to preserve 320 milliseconds of tactile contrast before the result. The visual clock anchors after Core Haptics starts, placing tactile contact at exact maximum compression 0.082 seconds into the 0.82-second winner landing; the low-sharpness body lasts 0.78 seconds and uses no synthesized tone. When Pinball needs its one fair flex, its springy cue is distinct from rendered ordinary-collision cues and the separate endpoint settle thud. The flexing contact may be a wall or a seat ring; a ring lights exactly as any struck ring does. The offline audio fallback remains available on supported physical devices when Core Haptics is unavailable; simulator startup is capability-gated and never starts AVAudioEngine.

## Optional TestFlight — skipped for version 1.0

TestFlight was intentionally skipped for the version 1.0 submission. It is an optional post-processing smoke test, not an App Store submission prerequisite. The following guidance remains available for a future internal build:

Please try all three modes in portrait and landscape:

• Chooser — place 2–5 fingers at once, including adding or lifting a finger during the 1.0-second stability window and 2.75-second synchronized ritual.
• Tap In — add groups of different sizes and try Undo, Clear, Try Again, and New group.
• Pinball — add 2–12 seats, try directional flicks, run repeated choices, and rotate or background the app during a run.

Please also check the intentionally text-free Chooser playfield; the 1.0-second stability window; the six-beat 2.75-second anticipation arc; and whether tactile contact feels locked to maximum winner compression at 0.082 seconds. In Pinball, confirm the ball appears beneath the finger only after flick commitment, stays attached through release, and launches from the exact clamped release point along the exact first leg. Natural matching paths should have no special effect. When modulation is needed, only a localized first-wall section should visibly flex and rebound with a distinct tactile signature; no text should appear, later bounces should remain ordinary, and the ball should stop in the visibly winning region. In each safe screen, make one quick simultaneous three-finger tap and confirm it selects a different saved world without changing the current mode, group, or result; it must not create a player, seat, button activation, launch, or dismissal. With VoiceOver enabled, use the Shuffle colors accessibility action instead and confirm VoiceOver's own three-finger gestures still work. Also verify all eight Settings worlds and persistence, the native Liquid Glass toolbar in portrait and landscape, all three App Shortcuts and deep links, Dynamic Type, Reduce Motion, Reduce Transparency, Increase Contrast, Differentiate Without Color, offline launch, launch-default migration v2 followed by persistent explicit defaults, immediate safe mode changes, result background taps, and the adaptive Five Seats, One Table Home Screen icon in its Default, Dark, Clear Light, Clear Dark, Tinted Dark, and monochrome system presentations. Send feedback to contact@foliohtml.com.
